Official Status and Origins

The only official language in Ukraine is Ukrainian. Ukrainian is an East Slavic language, spoken primarily in Ukraine. It is the native language of a large majority of Ukrainians. Written Ukrainian uses the Ukrainian alphabet, a variant of the Cyrillic script.

Ukrainian is a descendant of Old East Slavic, a language spoken in the medieval state of Kievan Rusʹ. During the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, the language developed into Ruthenian, where it was granted official language status.

Dialectal Groups

In the Ukrainian language, there are three major dialectal groups according to territory: the southwestern group of dialects, the southeastern group of dialects and the northern group of dialects.

Due to historical and geographical factors, there are so many dialects in each of these groups that almost every region of Ukraine has its own unique words and style of speech. The dialects spoken in the Cherkasy, Poltava, and Kyiv regions are considered to be the closest to standard Ukrainian.

History of Oppression and State Status

Throughout its history, Ukraine, located in the centre of Europe and between the major European powers, has suffered from being divided between numerous countries, including Mongolia, Lithuania, Poland, Russia, Türkiye, Austria, Hungary, Romania and others.

Until the 1990s, the Ukrainian language was banned or oppressed for approximately 376 years, subjected to repression or did not have full state status in those countries that included Ukrainian lands. In modern times, Ukrainian has had state status in Ukraine since October 27, 1989, when the corresponding amendments were made to the Constitution of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic.
