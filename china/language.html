Chinese language

Most people in China speak Mandarin. There is a multitude of dialects being spoken in China, e.g., Cantonese, Xiang, or Hakka just to name a few.
Mandarin is mainly spoken by people from the northern, central, and western parts of China. Cantonese, Xiang and Hakka are more commonly spoken by people in the southeastern part of China.

Despite differences in pronunciation, all the Chinese languages share the same literary language known as wenyan. This has no single standard of pronunciation hence one word can be pronounced in various ways according to the rules of pronunciation in the person's area.

Before the year 1917 Wenyan was the common language used for writing. Wenyan soon got replaced by Pinyin to ensure that every Chinese person could understand each other regardless of which region they're from. Pinyin is mainly used for identifying the sounds of Hanzi, which are Chinese characters.

Pinyin is like the English alphabet except it consists of 4 tones so people can distinguish between the meaning of Chinese characters that are spelt the same i.e. spelt with the same vowels and consonants. It acts as the modern standard language.

The 4 tones are:

  1. High-level tone
  2. High-rising tone
  3. Falling-rising tone
  4. Falling tone

Only vowels have tones, while consonants do not.

  1. Has a horizontal line on top of the pinyin vowel, known as a macron (ˉ) e.g. ā ē ê̄ ī ō ū ǖ Ā Ē Ê̄ Ī Ō Ū Ǖ.
  2. Has a diagonal line from right to left on top of the pinyin vowel, known as an acute accent (ˊ) e.g. á é ế í ó ú ǘ Á É Ế Í Ó Ú Ǘ.
  3. Has a V-shaped line on top of the pinyin vowel, known as a caron (ˇ) e.g. ǎ ě ê̌ ǐ ǒ ǔ ǚ Ǎ Ě Ê̌ Ǐ Ǒ Ǔ Ǚ.
  4. Has a diagonal line from left to right on top of the pinyin vowel, known as a grave accent (ˋ) e.g. à è ề ì ò ù ǜ À È Ề Ì Ò Ù Ǜ.

    5. There is also a neutral tone where the vowels have no accent marks at all meaning those words do not need to be stressed when pronouncing them.
