Chinese New Year

Chinese New Year (Chūn Jié) is the most salient festival of all times in China and usually falls between late January and mid-February. It's a time for celebration and family reunions. Chinese people, regardless of where they are living in, will strive to return to their family home to celebrate Chinese New Year with their families, relatives, and friends.

History of Chinese New Year

One of the most famous myths that elucidates the history of Chinese New Year would be the legend about a beast called "Nian". Nian survived by eating livestock, crops, and people on New Year's Eve. The villagers were terrified of Nian and often hid inside their house with food left out on the port for Nian to consume. Fortunately, one day, an old wise man discovered that Nian feared the colour red and loud noises. He provided a solution to prevent Nian from attacking the village. The people started decorating their houses with red lanterns, red scrolls, and setting off firecrackers to scare off the beast.

Events

During New Year's Eve, Chinese people thoroughly clean their house in hopes of sweeping away bad luck and welcoming good fortune. Children and unmarried adults receive red packets that have money inside them as a symbol for fortune. Red is the lucky colour for everyone in China since it represents good luck. Even the decorations and the clothes they wear are all red for this festival, e.g. they hang up red lanterns and do paper cutting, coloured red of course.

Traditional Foods

There are special dishes that Chinese people consume during this event. They each have a symbolic meaning for the year ahead.

  1. Glutinous rice balls are round in shape. This symbolizes unity and family togetherness.
  2. Rice cake (nián gāo) a sticky, white rice cake made from glutinous rice. The literal translation of nián gāo is "higher year". This symbolizes growth and progress, be it in the career field or academics.
  3. Fish (yú) symbolizes abundance due to the word "yú" sounding like the word "surplus" in Chinese.
  4. Dumplings (jiǎozi) has the same shape as ancient gold ingots in China. They symbolize fortune and prosperity.
